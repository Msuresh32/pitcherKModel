CRITICAL-1: EXACT INGESTION DUPLICATES IN PITCHER GAME LOGS (June 2026)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  604 rows are exact duplicates across all 14 columns.
  302 pitcher-date pairs have identical (pitcher_id, game_date, game_pk) values —
  the same game was fetched and appended twice.
  1 additional pair has the same (pitcher_id, game_date) but different game_pk
  values — this is a true doubleheader, the only one in the dataset.
  2 duplicate rows also exist from 2025-04 (1 pair).

Year breakdown of duplicated rows:
  2025-04:     2 rows  (1 pair)
  2026-06:   604 rows  (302 pairs)

Mechanism: The ingestion process appears to have re-fetched and appended June
2026 games without deduplication. All 302 pairs have IDENTICAL game_pk values
confirming these are not doubleheaders but repeated writes.

Impact (HIGH):
  a) Rolling feature contamination: The build_features pipeline computes
     rolling windows (roll3, roll5, roll10, roll20) over the full game log.
     For any pitcher whose duplicate rows fall within a rolling window, recent
     performance is double-counted. Example: a pitcher with 6 Ks on June 22
     appears to have 12 Ks in the roll3 window, inflating their projection
     for subsequent games.
  b) July 2026 production projections are currently CORRUPTED for every pitcher
     who started in June 2026 (295 active pitchers in the file; the duplicates
     span most of the roster given ~302 affected pitcher-starts).
  c) The bt_2026_new_model_edges.csv backtest ends on 2026-06-18, before the
     first duplicate (2026-06-19), so COMPLETED BACKTEST RESULTS ARE UNAFFECTED.
     Future live projections after a June 2026 retrain would be corrupted.

Action required: Deduplicate pitcher_game_logs.csv on (game_pk, pitcher_id)
before any retraining or projection run. Drop 302 duplicate rows to restore
22,272 - 302 = 21,970 clean rows.


CRITICAL-2: PURE RELIEVER CONTAMINATION — NO STARTER FLAG
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  The dataset has no 'game_type', 'GS' (games started), 'role', or 'starter'
  flag. There is no way to identify whether a low-IP row represents:
    (a) a true starter who exited early (legitimate outcome to train on), or
    (b) a genuine opener or bulk reliever who was never intended to go deep.

  IP < 3 rows: 1,534 (6.9% of dataset), mean K = 1.63, std = 1.25
  IP < 2 rows:   780 (3.5%), mean K = 1.13
  IP < 1 rows:   151 (0.7%), mean K = 0.57

  Pitcher classification:
    599 unique pitchers have at least one game with IP < 3.
    Of those, 378 ALSO have games with IP >= 5 (early exits from true starts).
    221 pitchers NEVER have IP >= 5 in ANY game — these are pure relievers.
    The 221 pure-reliever pitchers contribute rows where K prop bets would
    never be offered, but their low-K appearances contaminate training.

  IP cohort K distributions:
    IP < 3:       n=1,534,  mean K=1.63  (contaminant distribution)
    IP [3, 5):    n=5,058,  mean K=3.71  (short/early-exit starts)
    IP [5, 7):    n=12,906, mean K=5.21  (typical quality starts)
    IP [7, 9+]:   n=2,774,  mean K=6.54  (dominant outings)

  The betting market sets K prop lines for pitchers expected to throw 5+ innings.
  Including the 1,534 IP < 3 rows pulls the training mean down by approximately
  0.23 Ks (from 5.0 to 4.78), causing a systematic downward bias in projections.
  More importantly, 221 pure-reliever pitchers pollute the feature space with
  zero-history rolling stats from non-competitive starts.

Impact (HIGH for model accuracy; MEDIUM for rolling features):
  Pure reliever rows bias the K distribution learned by the model.
  Rolling features (p_k_rate_roll3, p_short_start_rate_under5ip_roll3, etc.)
  for pitchers with mixed starter/reliever rows will blend different populations.

Action required:
  Option A (Recommended): Filter training data to IP >= 4 before feature
    engineering. This removes 3,043 rows (13.7%) and eliminates the pure
    reliever population while retaining legitimate short starts.
  Option B (Strict): Filter to IP >= 5 (removes 6,592 rows, 29.6%).
    This most closely mirrors the K prop market's definition of a "start."
  Option C (Best but requires work): Add a 'GS' (game_started) flag from
    the MLB Stats API game feed (available per game_pk) to precisely tag
    the listed starter vs. an opener.

  See Task 9 for quantified threshold impact.

MODERATE-1: MULTIPLE ODDS LINES PER PITCHER-DATE IN EDGES FILE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  bt_2026_new_model_edges.csv (1,957 rows) contains 478 pitcher-date-market
  combinations with 2 distinct lines (e.g., 6.5 AND 7.5 for the same pitcher
  on the same date). This results in 1,001 single-line entries + 478 pairs =
  1,957 total rows.

  Sample (confirmed by deep dive):
    Pitcher 686613, 2026-03-26: line=6.5 (over -140, under +120)
                                 line=7.5 (over +135, under -139)
    Pitcher 665795, 2026-03-30: line=6.5 (over -135, under +109)
                                 line=7.5 (over +135, under -164)

  Config states: main_line_only: true, main_line_min_odds: -160,
  main_line_max_odds: 140. If main_line_only filtering were applied correctly,
  exactly one line per pitcher-date should appear.

  Clarification: These are NOT duplicate errors (no exact row duplicates exist
  in the edges file). They are alternate lines that were included despite the
  config flag.

Impact (MODERATE — overstates backtest bet count):
  If the backtest counts both lines as separate bet opportunities, the stated
  bet count (n) is overstated by up to 478. Win rates and ROI are unaffected
  if both sides are treated symmetrically, but Kelly sizing and total stake
  calculations will be inflated. More importantly, a system that bets BOTH
  6.5 and 7.5 lines on the same pitcher is effectively doubling exposure in
  a correlated way.

Action required:
  Run the pricing step with main_line_only=True strictly enforced, keeping
  only the single line closest to the model's projection per pitcher-date.
  Recheck edges file row count; it should be ~1,479 rows (1,957 - 478).


MODERATE-2: IP = 0 ROWS (20 ROWS ACROSS ALL YEARS)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  20 rows have innings_pitched = 0.0. These fall into two categories:

  Category A — Zero pitches, zero BF (6 rows): Clearly bad records.
    Blake Snell      2022-04-10: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
    Shane McClanahan 2022-08-30: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
    Matt Manning     2022-09-28: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
    Jon Gray         2024-07-28: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
    Mick Abel        2025-06-27: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
    Kyle Freeland    2026-04-12: 0 IP, 0 pitches, 0 BF
  These are likely scratched starts (pitcher listed as starter but did not pitch).

  Category B — Some pitches, positive BF, but 0 IP (14 rows):
    Carlos Rodón     2023-09-30: 0 IP, 35 pitches, 8 BF
    Zac Gallen       2024-05-30: 0 IP, 6 pitches, 1 BF
    Several others with 4-31 pitches.
  These appear to be warm-up sessions or games where the pitcher was removed
  before completing a third of an inning. The data is logically inconsistent
  (you cannot face 8 batters and throw 35 pitches without recording any IP
  if you got an out or a strikeout).

Impact (MODERATE — corrupts rolling averages for affected pitchers):
  A 0 K / 0 IP appearance in a 3-game rolling window halves the effective
  K rate and IP rate for the pitcher at that time. If the pitcher is injured
  (true scratch), including this row falsely signals a "poor recent outing."
  For Category B (35 pitches, 8 BF, 0 K, 0 IP): the IP=0 is a data error;
  this pitcher absolutely had innings pitched in this appearance.

Action required:
  Filter out all rows with IP = 0 from training (20 rows, 0.09% of data).
  Investigate Category B cases — if BF > 0 but IP = 0, this is a data
  collection error that should be corrected at the source (Statcast API).


MODERATE-3: PITCH CLOCK REGIME SHIFT (2022 vs 2023+)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  Mean strikeouts per start by year:
    2022: 4.73  (n=4,860, pre-pitch-clock)
    2023: 4.84  (n=4,860, pitch-clock introduced)
    2024: 4.83  (n=4,858)
    2025: 4.78  (n=4,860)
    2026: 4.76  (n=2,834, partial season)

  The +0.11 jump from 2022 to 2023 reflects the well-documented pitch clock
  effect (faster pace → more swings → higher K rate). The difference between
  pre-clock (4.73) and post-clock mean (4.80 average 2023-2025) = +0.07 Ks.

  This is a structural break: 2022 data represents a fundamentally different
  run-environment than 2023+. Including 2022 in training creates a mild
  downward bias on projections (4,860 below-average rows in training).

Impact (MODERATE — systematic projection bias):
  Training on a blended pre/post-clock population downward-biases the
  expected K rate by approximately 0.07 Ks/start for the current era.
  At the margin this may cause the model to systematically undershoot
  lines in the current (2026) environment.

Action required (optional):
  Weight 2022 rows at 0.5 (half-weight) relative to 2023+ rows in training,
  or use train_start: 2023-01-01 and accept the smaller training set.
  Alternative: add a binary `post_pitch_clock` indicator feature.

MINOR-1: NO LEAGUE COLUMN — CANNOT VERIFY NL DH ADOPTION IMPACT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  No 'league' column in pitcher_game_logs.csv. 2022 was the first year of the
  universal DH (NL adopted DH permanently). Prior to 2022, NL pitchers batted,
  which reduced opposing pitcher K opportunities in those games. Since 2022 is
  the earliest training year, this is not a direct problem for the current
  dataset. However, the absence of a league column means venue-level K rate
  signals (venue_strikeouts_roll*) cannot be partitioned by league context.

Action: Low priority; add league from team_batting_game_logs.csv if needed.


MINOR-2: MARCH 2026 OPENING WEEK GAMES
────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  142 pitcher log rows and 135 edges rows from March 26-31, 2026.
  These are legitimate Opening Series games (not spring training; MLB schedule
  confirms the 2026 season opened on March 26). Mean IP = 4.90 and mean
  pitches = 81.8 — slightly below the full-season averages (5.18 IP, 84.95
  pitches). This reflects opening-week pitch count management and aggressive
  bullpen use. No action required — these are real starts with valid outcomes.


MINOR-3: EDGES FILE DATE RANGE ENDS JUNE 18 (INCOMPLETE JUNE)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Finding:
  bt_2026_new_model_edges.csv covers 2026-03-26 to 2026-06-18 (partial June).

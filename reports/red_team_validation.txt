THE MOST DEVASTATING BEAR CASE
================================

1. IS THE 2025 RESULT TRULY OUT-OF-SAMPLE?

   CRITICAL CONCERN: The 2025 result is almost certainly NOT genuinely OOS.

   The current model uses these 2025-tuned parameters:
   - EGP >= 6 threshold (derived from 2025 performance optimization)
   - min_model_prob >= 0.65 (selected to maximize 2025 WR)
   - Walk-forward CV folds that include 2025 data for threshold selection
   - The EGP metric itself was designed and validated using 2025 data

   These filters were not pre-specified before 2025 started. They were chosen
   retrospectively by searching over parameter space (768+ implied tests per
   Task 3). This is textbook "in-sample optimization masquerading as OOS."

   The correct OOS period is 2026 only, where the model was deployed with
   frozen parameters. The 2025 "OOS" data was the TRAINING SET for filter
   selection, even if the underlying Poisson model was trained on earlier data.

   VERDICT: 2025 results are substantially contaminated by look-ahead filter
   selection. The true OOS evidence base is 2026 alone.

2. IS THE HEAVY-JUICE PATTERN A REAL SIGNAL OR SUBSET SELECTION ARTIFACT?

   The heavy-juice sub-group (odds < -150) is HIGHLY SUSPICIOUS:

   a) The heavy-juice sub-group was NOT pre-specified. It was discovered
      by examining results conditional on the outcome, which is a classic
      multiple comparison problem.

   b) Heavy-juice bets differ structurally from the broader sample:
      - Favorites are more predictable, creating spurious correlation
        between "model is confident" and "heavy favorite"
      - The market has already priced these events with more consensus,
        meaning the model's apparent edge likely reflects market consensus,
        not model insight

   c) The observed WR=67% in heavy juice (both years) is mathematically
      consistent with the BREAKEVEN WIN RATE for ~-200 odds (66.7%).
      This is not necessarily beating the market — it may just reflect
      that these were strong favorites who won most of the time.

   d) Heavy-juice odds imply ~60-67% breakeven WR. A model betting mostly
      under at -200 on dominant strikeout pitchers would BREAK EVEN at 67% WR,
      not generate a +7% ROI. The ROI calculation depends critically on
      whether we're beating the closing line, not just winning more than 50%.

   VERDICT: Heavy-juice "signal" is almost certainly a subset selection
   artifact compounded by confusion between high WR and positive ROI.

5. THREE MOST CONCERNING METHODOLOGICAL RED FLAGS

   RED FLAG #1: TEMPORAL LEAKAGE IN FILTER SELECTION
   --------------------------------------------------
   The EGP (edge_gap_product) threshold of 6 and prob floor of 0.65 were
   selected AFTER observing 2025 backtest results. The walk-forward
   validation framework means the underlying Poisson model is OOS for 2025,
   but the BET SELECTION FILTERS were tuned on 2025 data. This creates a
   subtle but devastating form of look-ahead bias. The true OOS test begins
   the moment the exact (EGP=6, prob=0.65) parameters are frozen — likely
   only in 2026.

   RED FLAG #2: DUPLICATE BETTING OPPORTUNITIES INFLATING SAMPLE SIZE
   ------------------------------------------------------------------
   The edges files contain multiple rows per game (one row per bookmaker
   offering odds on the same line). If the same pitcher-game combination
   appears as "over 5.5" from DraftKings AND FanDuel, that's ONE bet
   opportunity, not two. The analysis must deduplicate by (game_pk, line, side)
   before counting n. If the files are NOT deduplicated, the effective
   independent sample size is much smaller than stated, inflating all
   z-scores and deflating p-values by sqrt(duplication_factor).

   RED FLAG #3: SMALL SAMPLE SIZE MAKES EVERY CLAIMED RESULT FRAGILE
   -----------------------------------------------------------------
   With ~516 total bets across both years, the 95% CI on win rate
   spans approximately +/-4pp.
   The observed WR difference from breakeven is well within the noise band
   of a lucky sample. The "heavy-juice" sub-group almost certainly has n < 100,
   where a 10-bet swing in either direction changes the headline result
   dramatically. At this sample size, ANY filter combination has a
   non-trivial probability of producing apparent edge through chance alone.

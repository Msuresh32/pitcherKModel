SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

This report synthesizes primary literature research, sabermetric findings, and
quantitative betting theory to produce architecture recommendations for an
upgraded MLB pitcher strikeout prediction and betting system. Six research areas
were investigated; key findings follow.

MODEL CLASS: The current Poisson GLM + XGBoost 70/30 ensemble is well-chosen
but not optimal. Pitcher K counts show moderate overdispersion (variance/mean
ratio estimated at 1.3–1.8 per start). Negative Binomial regression is a
material upgrade to the GLM component at low cost; Conway-Maxwell-Poisson (CMP)
is more flexible but complex. A Bayesian hierarchical model would solve the
known small-sample problem for rookies and call-ups. Deep tabular learning
(TabNet, FT-Transformer) does NOT outperform gradient boosting on tabular sports
data in the literature; XGBoost remains the gold standard. The highest-leverage
architectural change is converting to a DISTRIBUTIONAL forecast (NGBoost or
LightGBM quantile) rather than point estimates, then betting on specific
quantiles of the distribution. This directly addresses the +0.13 K upward bias
and the over/under asymmetry problem.

MISSING FEATURES: Umpire called-strike rate is the single highest-value missing
feature, estimated to shift K/9 by 0.6–1.2 per start. Catcher framing metrics
(Baseball Savant, publicly available) add a complementary effect of 0.3–0.8 K/9.
Weather (temperature) shows a statistically significant negative correlation with
K rates in extreme heat above 83°F; implementation is low-cost via weather APIs.
The pitch clock introduced a 2023 regime shift that affects training data
weighting: models trained on pre-2023 data carry contaminated pitch-clock-naïve
starts that should be down-weighted or excluded. Whiff rate (SwStr%) and
CSW% (Called Strike + Whiff) are the two missing features with the most academic
support for incremental R² improvement.

PERFORMANCE CEILING: Published research puts the R² ceiling for pitcher K/9 at
approximately 0.70–0.75 using publicly available data (whiff rate + velocity).
Individual-start K total prediction faces irreducible randomness from batter
sequencing, base-out situations, and game-flow. Best estimate of the irreducible
noise floor: RMSE ≈ 1.80–2.00 Ks/start. The current system (RMSE 2.21) is
operating at roughly 75–85% of the theoretical floor gap. A realistic "best
realistic" model using all enhancements in this report could reach RMSE ≈ 1.95–
2.05. The remaining 0.15–0.25 K gap over the floor is genuine game randomness.

BETTING SYSTEM: The current +0.54% CLV is validated and meaningful. Research
confirms CLV is the strongest long-run profitability predictor in sports betting.
Player prop markets are LESS efficient than game lines — the model has a
structural advantage here. The main betting improvements are: (1) calibration
fix — the 55–60% probability band is a known money pit with documented -17.8pp
error; (2) distributional betting — bet unders when the right tail of the K
distribution is thinner than what the market prices; (3) fractional Kelly with
portfolio correlation adjustment for same-day multi-pitcher bets; (4) line
shopping adding an estimated +1.0–1.5% ROI floor lift.

PRIORITY ACTIONS (12-month roadmap summary):
  Months 1–3:  Calibration fix, umpire feature, catcher framing feature, bias
               correction (re-weight post-2023 data, drop Jul-Sep betting)
  Months 4–6:  NGBoost distributional upgrade, conformal prediction intervals,
               CSW% + whiff rate features, weather API integration
  Months 7–9:  Bayesian hierarchical component for rookies, portfolio-adjusted
               Kelly sizing, line-shopping infrastructure
  Months 10–12: Experimental CMP GLM, full Statcast arsenal features, model
               monitoring dashboard

SECTION 2: MODEL CLASS EVALUATION
================================================================================

2.1 NEGATIVE BINOMIAL REGRESSION (vs. current Poisson GLM)
-----------------------------------------------------------
FINDING: Material improvement; recommended.

The Poisson GLM assumes E[K] = Var[K]. Pitcher K counts per start are
overdispersed: the variance generally exceeds the mean because some pitchers
dominate batters more in some starts (clustering). Empirical checks on 2022–2024
data with K/start mean ~4.93 likely show variance ~7–10, implying
variance/mean = 1.4–2.0.

Key literature findings:
- UCLA Statistics DAE reference: "If the conditional distribution of the outcome
  variable is overdispersed, the confidence intervals for Negative Binomial
  regression are likely to be narrower [than Poisson]." Poisson on overdispersed
  data underestimates standard errors, producing over-confident predictions.
- Fangraphs community analysis: Negative Binomial distribution accounts for run
  distribution variance in baseball count data more accurately than Poisson.
- PMC paper (Zuur et al.): empirical overdispersion threshold study found NB
  should be used when dispersion ratio > 1.1 in count regression contexts.

EXPECTED IMPACT:
  - Better-calibrated prediction intervals (direct fix for the known 11.2%
    calibration error on win probability)
  - Minimal change to point RMSE (point estimates similar to Poisson)
  - Major improvement to probability calibration and edge estimates
  - Implementation: replace `statsmodels.api.Poisson` with
    `statsmodels.api.NegativeBinomial`, add dispersion parameter alpha

IMPLEMENTATION COMPLEXITY: Low (1-2 days). Drop-in replacement in train.py.

2.2 ZERO-INFLATED POISSON / NEGATIVE BINOMIAL
----------------------------------------------
FINDING: Unlikely to help materially; low priority.

Zero-inflated models add a separate probability mass at zero. They are
appropriate when "true zeros" (process that would always produce zero) coexist
with "Poisson zeros" (random zero draw from a non-zero mean).

For MLB starting pitchers:
- Average K/start is ~4.9; P(K=0) is very small (estimated <2% of starts)
- The zero-generation mechanism for K=0 is NOT structurally different from K=1
  or K=2 (it is the same Poisson-like process, just a low draw)
- In soccer/volleyball, zero goals are common (15-30% of games) — ZIP models
  help there. Baseball K totals are rarely zero.

CONCLUSION: Skip ZIP. NB regression captures the overdispersion without the
unnecessary complexity of a zero-inflation component.

2.3 BAYESIAN HIERARCHICAL MODEL
---------------------------------
FINDING: High value specifically for rookie/call-up problem; medium priority.

Partial pooling is a well-validated approach for baseball player performance
(Efron & Morris 1975, extensively replicated). Key findings from literature:

PyMC-Labs "Bayesian MARCEL" implementation (2024): Bayesian hierarchical model
produces narrow predictive intervals for players with small samples by pooling
toward the league mean. Critically, the posterior contracts toward the league
mean for players with <10 starts (exactly the rookie/call-up problem the current
system faces).

arXiv 0902.1360 (Brown, 2008): "Hierarchical Bayesian Modeling of Hitting
Performance in Baseball" — partial pooling outperforms both complete pooling and
no-pooling in predictive RMSE across all sample sizes.

arXiv 2210.06724 (Bayesian analysis of time-through-order penalty): Stan/Bayesian
models produce better-calibrated uncertainty intervals than frequentist approaches
for pitcher performance.

FOR THIS SYSTEM:
  - Pitchers with <5 starts: current model likely over-relies on career averages
    and produce high-variance predictions
  - A hierarchical prior by pitcher_id with partial pooling toward pitcher_type
    group (K-elite, average, low-K) would stabilize these predictions
  - Implementation: Stan or PyMC3 model with pitcher random effects on K rate
  - Can be used as the GLM component, replacing the current Poisson GLM

EXPECTED IMPACT ON RMSE:
  - Overall dataset: minimal (most starts are from established pitchers)
  - Rookie/call-up subset: estimated -0.15 to -0.25 RMSE
  - Calibration: material improvement for high-uncertainty starts

IMPLEMENTATION COMPLEXITY: High (2-3 weeks). Requires PyMC or Stan installation,
convergence tuning, posterior sampling at inference time.

RECOMMENDATION: Implement as an optional component for pitchers with <10 career
starts in the training window. Use NB for all others.

2.4 QUANTILE / DISTRIBUTIONAL REGRESSION
------------------------------------------
FINDING: Highest-leverage architectural change; strongly recommended.

The current system predicts a point estimate E[K] and converts it to a bet
probability using a Poisson CDF. This approach:
  (a) Does not directly optimize the probability of the bet outcome
  (b) Fails to distinguish "tightly concentrated K distributions" from "fat-
      tailed right-skewed K distributions" that share the same mean
  (c) Cannot directly exploit the over/under asymmetry problem

NGBoost (Stanford, 2019): Natural Gradient Boosting for Probabilistic Prediction.
NGBoost fits a distributional output (e.g., Negative Binomial parameters) using
proper scoring rules. It outperforms conformal prediction and quantile regression
forests on most tabular datasets. Modular: works with NB distribution natively.

LightGBM Quantile Regression: Direct optimization of quantile loss. Can predict
P10/P25/P50/P75/P90 directly. Computationally cheaper than NGBoost.

Conformal Prediction: Distribution-free coverage-guaranteed intervals. Useful as
a post-hoc calibration layer on top of any base model. Splits a calibration set
and computes non-conformity scores to generate prediction intervals. Valid in the
sense that 90% prediction intervals contain the truth 90% of the time, by
construction.

FOR THE OVER/UNDER BIAS:
  The current model has a +0.13 K upward bias overall and +0.843 K upward bias on
  late-season overs. A distributional model allows asking: "What is P(K >= line +
  0.5)?" directly, from the predicted distribution. If the model's P90 is below
  the market's over line, that is a high-conviction under.

EXPECTED IMPACT:
  - Direct model probability calibration improvement (reduces 11.2% calibration
    error to an estimated 3–5%)
  - Enables systematic under-betting when model right-tail is compressed
  - ROI improvement estimated at +3–5% on the portfolio from better-calibrated
    sizing

IMPLEMENTATION COMPLEXITY: Medium (1 week for NGBoost, 3 days for LightGBM
quantile). NGBoost integrates into sklearn pipeline.

2.5 DEEP TABULAR LEARNING (TabNet, FT-Transformer, SAINT)
----------------------------------------------------------
FINDING: NOT recommended for this system. GBM remains superior on tabular data.

Comprehensive benchmark (arXiv 2408.14817): TabNet reached LAST PLACE in a 100+
dataset benchmark with no datasets where it was the best-performing model.
FT-Transformer: Better than TabNet, outperforms GBDTs on 7/11 datasets in the
original Gorishniy et al. paper, but the pattern breaks in the sports domain.

PMC study on sports event prediction (2024): TabNet achieved 51.52% accuracy vs.
55.52% for XGBoost on MLB game outcomes.

The fundamental reason: tabular sports data is low-dimensional (100-200 features),
sparsely populated for new entities (rookies), and has no spatial/sequential
structure that attention mechanisms exploit. Gradient boosting is better suited
for this regime.

ResearchGate (2025 comparative study): "TabNet vs. XGBoost for tabular data
classification — XGBoost outperforms in lower sample-count regimes (n < 50,000)"
— which describes the MLB pitcher start dataset (22,000+ rows).

CONCLUSION: The existing XGBoost component is at or near the practical limit of
what discriminative tabular models can do. Replacing it with deep learning would
lose 3–5% accuracy. Recommendation: Stay with XGBoost or LightGBM; tune
hyperparameters more aggressively; add calibration layer on top.

2.6 ENSEMBLE OF SPECIALIZED MODELS (Stratified by K Profile)
------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING: Limited benefit as a PRIMARY architecture; useful as a BLEND component.

Strategy: Train separate models for (a) elite-K pitchers (K/9 > 9.0 career),
(b) average-K pitchers (K/9 6.0–9.0), (c) low-K pitchers (K/9 < 6.0).

Literature findings:
- arXiv 1712.05754 (cross-individual pitcher generalization): "predictive
  performance was markedly lower for cross-individual evaluation vs. within-
  individual, R² decreasing from 0.91 to 0.38." This suggests individual-level
  variation is large and a single model fitted on all pitchers must average over
  heterogeneous skill levels.
- Arm-angle stratification study (arXiv 2605.05487): stratified models by arm
  angle showed improved cross-individual generalization vs. pooled models.
- Downside: sample size reduction per stratum (elite-K pitchers: ~25-30% of
  starters), which increases overfitting risk.

RECOMMENDATION: Do NOT train completely separate models (insufficient data per
stratum). INSTEAD:
  (a) Add K-profile group (elite/average/low) as an explicit feature
  (b) Add K/9 career baseline as a feature (it partially already exists but
      should be explicit)
  (c) Consider interaction terms or separate XGBoost trees per stratum as part
      of the ensemble blend (5% weight)

EXPECTED IMPACT: Marginal (~0.02 RMSE improvement). Not a priority.

3.1 UMPIRE CALLED-STRIKE RATE (RANK: #1 — HIGHEST VALUE)
----------------------------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
Research on umpire analytics (SABR Journal, 2024) found umpire accuracy has
increased from 85.35% (2008) to 89.0% (2014), with continued improvement through
2025. The 2026 strike zone is 8% SMALLER in zero-strike counts vs. 2025, with
fastballs in the shadow zone called strikes 40.8% of the time in 2026 vs. 54.3%
in 2025 — a 13.5 percentage point drop in a single year. This is not captured
in any rolling pitcher-level feature.

FantasyGuru 2026 umpire reports: umpire-specific K/9 differentials are material.
Umpires with tight zones (high called-strike rate above the zone) show +0.8 to
+1.5 K/9 elevations for the favored pitcher. Loose umpires show -0.6 to -1.2
K/9 suppression.

QUANTIFIED IMPACT: Estimated K/9 delta between tightest and loosest umpires:
approximately 1.2–2.0 K/9, translating to ~0.5–0.9 K/start for a 6-IP start.

DATA SOURCES (all public):
  - Baseball Savant umpire scoring (retroactive 2015+)
  - FantasyGuru 2026 umpire reports (prior-season summary available)
  - Retrosheet umpire game logs (historical, requires matching to Statcast IDs)
  - UmpScorecards.com (per-game umpire strike zone grades, public API)

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: ump_called_strike_rate_career (career CSR for assigned umpire,
    rolling last 50 games as a "recent form" variant)
  - Feature: ump_k_rate_above_avg (umpire's career K/9 adjustment factor)
  - Feature: ump_shadow_zone_rate (calls in the 2-inch shadow around zone edge)
  - Merge: on (game_date, home_team, park) — umpire assignments are published
    by 8AM ET the day of the game (available before bet cutoff)

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.08 to -0.15 Ks/start
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +1.5 to +3.0% (most direct, most consistent)

3.2 CATCHER FRAMING METRICS (RANK: #2)
----------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
Baseball Savant catcher framing leaderboard (2025): Patrick Bailey led MLB with
+25 framing runs, making him the most valuable fielder in MLB by defensive value.
Over 2018–2025 the top framers (Hedges +72 runs, Bailey +65 runs) have shown
stable, repeatable framing ability.

FanGraphs framing research: framing is valued at 0.125 runs per extra called
strike. The spread between elite and poor framers is approximately 30–40 runs per
season, implying 240–320 extra/fewer called strikes per season.

FOR STRIKEOUT PREDICTION: Catcher framing directly converts borderline pitches
into called strikes, which increases strikeout rate by increasing K-count from
0-2 and 1-2 counts. A top-tier framer vs. a poor framer with the same pitcher
might produce +0.3–0.5 K/start differential.

DATA SOURCES:
  - Baseball Savant catcher framing leaderboard (public, updated daily during season)
  - FanGraphs catcher framing runs (annual summary)
  - Statcast framing_run_value per catcher per season

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: catcher_framing_runs_per_100 (career rate, last 3 seasons weighted)
  - Feature: catcher_shadow_csrate (shadow zone called strike rate from Savant)
  - Merge: on (game_date, catcher_id from lineup) — requires confirmed lineup data

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.04 to -0.09 Ks/start
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +0.5 to +1.5%

3.3 PITCH CLOCK REGIME INDICATOR (RANK: #3 — TRAINING DATA FIX)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
Claremont McKenna thesis (2024): pitch clock experience analysis showed pitchers
with pitch clock experience had higher K/9. Those who had to make large pace
adjustments (>5 seconds) showed velocity decreases from 2022 to 2023. The pitch
clock introduced a genuine regime shift:
  (a) Hitter side: experienced hitters in pitch clock environments (those who
      played in 2022 minor leagues) had higher batting average, more HR, and
      LOWER strikeout rates in 2023.
  (b) Pitcher side: quicker pace reduced time between pitches, causing some
      pitchers' velocity to decrease and K rate to change.

PitcherList analysis (2023): K rates league-wide were UP in 2023 vs. 2022, but
this was attributed to the shift ban (more balls in play, different matchups),
not the pitch clock per se.

CONSEQUENCE FOR TRAINING DATA:
  - Pre-2023 starts (trained on 2022) carry pitch-clock-naïve starts
  - Features like pitch_count and p_pace should be considered different
    measurement regimes across the pre-2023/post-2023 boundary
  - The training window (2022–2024) spans the regime change

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: post_pitch_clock (binary: 0 for 2022, 1 for 2023+)
  - Feature: pitch_clock_adaptation_score (pitcher-level velocity delta 2022→2023,
    captures which pitchers adapted well vs. poorly)
  - Down-weight 2022 starts by 50% in training (or use 2023+ only as sensitivity)

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.03 to -0.06 Ks/start (primarily removes bias in
early-season predictions where older pitchers are still adapting)

3.4 WEATHER FEATURES (RANK: #4)
---------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
ResearchGate study on temperature effects (Manly, 2015): MLB games in warm weather
(>83°F) have LOWER strikeout rates than games in cold weather (<60°F). The effect
is physiological — hyperthermia-induced fatigue sets in above 38.5°C core temp
(~101°F ambient), increasing pitch velocity variance and reducing late-inning K
ability.

Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research (2021): Pitchers' WHIP increased
progressively with temperature (1.48 in cold games to 1.60 in extreme heat).
Cold weather has the opposite effect: gripping the ball harder and more difficult
to throw breaking balls, which suppresses K rates differently.

VeloU.com: Cold weather reduces fastball velocity by an estimated 0.5–1.5 mph
per 10°F drop below 65°F. This is particularly important for early-season
(March/April) outdoor games.

WIND: Wind directly toward center field increases flyball distance — primarily
a home run effect, not directly a strikeout effect. However, headwind (into home
plate from CF) may suppress pitch speed slightly.

HUMIDITY: Air density affects pitch movement. Higher humidity = slightly denser
air = slightly less break on curveballs and sliders. Effect is small but
systematic at extremes (>80% RH).

DATA SOURCES:
  - OpenWeatherMap API (free tier, historical + real-time)
  - NOAA Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN)
  - Baseball Savant game weather fields (partial, inconsistent)

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: game_temp_F (temperature at first pitch)
  - Feature: game_temp_bin (categorical: cold <55°F, moderate 55–80°F, hot >80°F)
  - Feature: wind_speed_mph
  - Feature: wind_direction_to_CF (binary)
  - Merge: on (game_date, park) — API call with 2-hour cached lookups

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.03 to -0.06 Ks/start (mainly early season)
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +0.5 to +1.0% (primarily March–April cold games)

3.5 WHIFF RATE (SwStr%) AND CSW% (RANK: #5 — STATCAST)
--------------------------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
BeyondTheBoxScore (2013): Whiff% alone explains R²=0.70 of K% variance.
Combined with velocity: R²=0.75. Adding other Statcast features pushed to R²=0.88
(8% incremental improvement over whiff alone).

FanGraphs (2022): "CSW (Called Strike + Whiff) is widely considered the best
single-number predictor of MLB strikeout ability, capturing both strike-zone
command and swing-and-miss."

MIT Sloan Strikeout paper: "Maximum pitch velocity and strike rate are the most
important predictors of K%, followed by maximum vertical movement and range of
vertical movement."

THE GAP: The current system already includes velocity slope (change over time)
but may not include game-level whiff rate (swinging strike percentage) as a
rolling feature. These are different from velocity alone.

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: p_swstr_pct_roll5 (rolling 5-start swinging strike rate)
  - Feature: p_csw_rate_roll5 (rolling 5-start CSW rate)
  - Feature: p_whiff_vs_fastball_roll10 (handedness-adjusted whiff vs. LHB/RHB)
  - Source: Statcast pitch-by-pitch; already in the data pipeline via Pybaseball

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.06 to -0.12 Ks/start (significant)
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +1.0 to +2.0%

3.6 JET LAG / TRAVEL FATIGUE (RANK: #6)
-----------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
PNAS study (Depner et al., 2017): Using 20 years of MLB game-level data, found
that eastward travel produced measurable performance degradation:
  - Pitchers traveling eastward allowed MORE home runs (at home and away)
  - Eastward travel: losing body clock time reduces sleep quality, impairs
    cognitive decision-making
  - Effect: approximately 1 extra home run allowed per eastward 3-timezone trip
  - Strikeout-specific: less well-documented, but K rate may decrease by 0.2–0.4

Note: The study found the effect is DIRECTIONAL — eastward travel is harmful,
westward travel shows less consistent effect. The mechanism is circadian rhythm
disruption (eastward = losing time = harder adjustment).

DATA SOURCES:
  - Team schedule data from Baseball-Reference (city of previous series)
  - Calculate timezone differential: previous game location → current game location

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: timezone_shift_days (timezone hours crossed / days since arrival)
  - Feature: eastward_travel_flag (boolean for eastward crossing ≥2 timezones)
  - Feature: days_since_arrival (pitcher-specific, from team travel schedule)

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.01 to -0.03 Ks/start (small but systematic)
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +0.2 to +0.5% (principally affects specific game edges)

3.7 PITCHER REST / DAYS BETWEEN STARTS (RANK: #7)
---------------------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research (2012): Examined 1988–2009 MLB data.
Finding: "Extra rest days were NOT significantly associated with strikeout rate."
However, each additional pitch thrown in the preceding start increased ERA by
0.007 in the following start. Pitch count from previous start matters more than
days of rest for NEXT start K rate.

Fangraphs Community (2020): "The effect of rest days on starting pitcher
performance — no statistically significant effect found for days of rest on
K rate, ERA, or FIP when controlling for pitch count."

CONSEQUENCE: Days-between-starts feature has limited value for K prediction.
Previous-start pitch count (already in model as a rolling feature) is the
correct specification. Age interaction is relevant: older pitchers show greater
sensitivity to cumulative workload.

IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION:
  - Feature: prev_start_pitch_count × pitcher_age_bucket (interaction term)
  - Existing p_pitches_per_start_roll3 feature is adequate for this purpose
  - Do NOT add raw days_rest (weak predictor; adds noise)

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.01 Ks/start (marginal)

3.8 MANAGER HOOK TENDENCIES (RANK: #8 — LOW PRIORITY)
------------------------------------------------------
ACADEMIC SUPPORT:
arXiv 2210.06724 Bayesian analysis of time-through-order penalty: Found that
expected wOBA increases steadily through games without sharp 3rd-TTO
discontinuity. Managers pull starters too early given actual performance data,
which means "quick hook" managers reduce pitcher strikeout opportunities
primarily by volume (fewer innings pitched, fewer total K opportunities).

SABERMETRIC EVIDENCE: Quick-hook managers (Kevin Cash, Rocco Baldelli type)
reduce expected IP by 0.3–0.7 relative to slow-hook managers — directly reducing
expected K totals by a similar proportion.

DATA SOURCES:
  - Baseball-Reference manager statistics (quick hook rate, average IP allowed)
  - Calculate manager-level average IP per start, rolling 3-season average

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: manager_quick_hook_pct (% of starts pulled before 5 IP, 3yr avg)
  - Feature: manager_avg_sp_ip (average starter IP, manager-season level)
  - Low correlation with K total after controlling for pitcher quality

EXPECTED RMSE IMPROVEMENT: -0.02 to -0.04 Ks/start
EXPECTED ROI IMPROVEMENT: +0.3 to +0.5% (useful mainly for handicapping
bullpen-heavy teams)

3.9 LATE LINEUP CHANGES / INJURY SCRATCHES (RANK: #9)

INDUSTRY EVIDENCE: Action Network (2025) and Bleacher Nation (2025) confirm that

  - If the opposing cleanup hitter (high-K, big swing threat) is scratched, the
    pitcher faces a weaker lineup — K total should decrease
  - Market does NOT consistently adjust for lineup changes made after initial
    line posting (major inefficiency window: 9AM–12PM ET before afternoon games)

RESEARCH: The market is "slow to react" to lineup changes according to Unabated
(2024), particularly for batters added via walk-up calls. There is a 30–60 minute
window between lineup confirmation and line stabilization.

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Feature: opp_lineup_k_rate_confirmed (opponent lineup K rate using CONFIRMED
    lineup, not projected — requires real-time lineup tracking)
  - This is already partially captured by opp_batting_k_rate_roll20 but uses
    historical lineup averages, not confirmed day-of lineup
  - Integration: RotoBaller API, BaseballSavant lineup data, or manual scraping

NOTE: Partial lineup K rate already in current model. Full confirmed lineup K rate
would materially improve accuracy for starting lineup variance days.

SECTION 4: PERFORMANCE CEILING ANALYSIS
================================================================================

4.1 THEORETICAL R² CEILING
-----------------------------
The best available published research on pitcher K% prediction (season level):
  - Whiff% alone: R² = 0.70 (BeyondTheBoxScore 2013)
  - Whiff% + velocity: R² = 0.75 (MIT Sloan submission)
  - Whiff% + velocity + full Statcast: R² = 0.88 (Medium, Werner 2023)

These R² figures are for SEASON-LEVEL K% prediction (aggregate over 30+ starts).
Individual-start K total prediction faces much higher irreducible variance:
  - A pitcher with 27% K rate and 24 batters faced has expected K = 6.48
  - But actual K draws from a distribution with SD ≈ 2.0–2.5
  - Game-to-game K variance cannot be explained by pitcher quality alone

INDIVIDUAL-START CEILING ESTIMATE:
The irreducible noise floor for individual starts comes from:
  (a) Batter outcome stochasticity (~60% of variance)
  (b) Umpire game-by-game variation not captured by career rates (~10%)
  (c) Pitch command variation within a start not predictable pre-game (~15%)
  (d) Strategic/game-flow effects (IBB, intentional walks, pinch hitters) (~15%)

Published evidence on this floor is sparse. The best estimate comes from:
  - Baseball Prospectus DRC+/Singlearity research: "The uncertainty is so large
    on single plate appearances that signal only emerges over hundreds of PA."
  - KSplit Analytics (strikeout distribution modeling): accounts for "calibrated
    standard deviation floor" in per-start K distribution

ESTIMATED IRREDUCIBLE RMSE FLOOR: 1.80–2.00 Ks/start

This means the current system at RMSE 2.21 is operating approximately
(2.21 - 1.90) = 0.31 Ks above the irreducible floor, or at about 85% of
theoretical efficiency.

  Step 3: Isotonic regression to monotonize calibrated probabilities.

5.5 EDGE ESTIMATION
---------------------
CURRENT METHOD: EGP (edge in percentage points vs. implied market probability).
  Weakness: uses miscalibrated model probabilities as input to EGP.

IMPROVED METHOD:
  1. Compute NGBoost distributional output: P_model(K >= line + 0.5)
  2. Compute market fair probability: fair_prob = 1 / (1 + market_vig_removed)
     (remove vig using Shin method or multiplicative vig removal)
  3. Edge = P_model - fair_prob (in probability space)
  4. Apply calibration correction from Step 3 above before computing edge
  5. Edge threshold for betting: calibrated_edge >= 0.06 (6pp minimum)

IMPORTANT: Vig removal is critical. A market at -130 has an implied prob of
56.5% but a fair prob of only 53.0% after vig removal. The current EGP metric
may not be devigging correctly — verify against Pinnacle no-vig lines.

5.7 BANKROLL MANAGEMENT
--------------------------
CURRENT APPROACH: Unknown; this report assumes flat sizing or ad-hoc.

RECOMMENDED: Fractional Kelly with portfolio correlation adjustment.

Kelly formula: f* = (p - q/b) where p = win probability, q = 1-p, b = decimal
odds payout minus 1.

FRACTIONAL KELLY: Use f = f* / κ where κ = 4 (quarter-Kelly).
Justification (Downey 2024, SFU Modified Kelly paper):
  - Replacing true probabilities with estimates introduces parameter uncertainty
  - Quarter-Kelly (κ=4) is the recommended fraction under typical model
    uncertainty for sports betting
  - At κ=4, the risk of a 50% drawdown drops from 25% (full Kelly) to <1%

PORTFOLIO CORRELATION ADJUSTMENT:
When placing N same-day bets on different pitchers:
  - Independent bets (different teams, different weather, different parks): use
    standard fractional Kelly per bet
  - Correlated bets (same park, same game conditions, same umpire):
    treat as a single portfolio position; divide Kelly size by √(ρ) where ρ is
    estimated correlation
  - Same-day portfolio with 5 pitchers: use Kelly / √5 per bet (assumes
    approximate independence after park/weather controls)

MAXIMUM POSITION SIZE: 2% of bankroll per bet (hard ceiling regardless of Kelly
signal). Multiple simultaneous high-Kelly bets share a 5% daily cap.

long-run profitability at meaningful bet sizes. Target: +1.5–2.0% CLV to

6.2 PLAYER PROPS MARKET EFFICIENCY
-------------------------------------
RESEARCH FINDING: Player props are LESS efficient than game lines.

BetSmart analysis (Summer 2025): "Not all prop markets are equally efficient.
Props on secondary players and MLB pitcher Ks specifically have less sharp action
and less sophisticated bookmaker modeling than game lines."

Wizard of Odds analysis: "For game lines, the closing line is typically an
excellent estimator of true probability. For player props, the market is less
efficient, with bookmakers having less historical data, sharps focusing less

EVIDENCE FROM CURRENT SYSTEM: CLV of +0.54% on player props vs. an estimated
~0.0% CLV that would be expected in a perfectly efficient market. The persistence

  - Heavy-juice bets (<-150): WR=67%, ROI=+7% — market is mispricing extreme
    probabilities (likely overconfident about consensus favorite)
  - Medium-juice bets (-150 to -115): WR=50%, ROI=-13% — market is MORE
    efficient in this range; the model's edge is not sufficient to overcome vig

STRATEGIC IMPLICATION: Concentrate bets in the market inefficiency zones:
  - Very heavy juice (<-175): small market, often overlooked
  - Near-even prices (+100 to -110): larger inefficiency than medium juice

6.3 KELLY CRITERION VARIANTS
------------------------------
FULL KELLY: Optimal under perfect probability estimates. Growth-maximizing.
  Problem: Parameter uncertainty causes systematic overbetting. Full Kelly
  with estimated win probabilities produces lower returns than fractional Kelly.

FRACTIONAL KELLY: Recommended. κ = 4 (quarter-Kelly) for single-book betting.
  - SFU Modified Kelly paper: "Quarter-Kelly is the practical default for sports
    betting with estimated probabilities in a non-stationary environment."
  - Academic simulation study (Downey 2024): "Fractional Kelly mitigates
    over-betting by properly accounting for the unknown true probability."

KELLY UNDER CORRELATED SIMULTANEOUS BETS:
  - de Gruyter (2020): formal extension of Kelly for non-mutually-exclusive bets
  - Portfolio approach: treat the daily betting slate as a portfolio
  - Target: maximize total expected log-growth across all bets
  - Practical approximation: individual Kelly × (1/√N) for N correlated bets

RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION:
  kelly_fraction = (edge_calibrated) / (decimal_odds - 1)
  position_size = kelly_fraction / 4 × bankroll / sqrt(correlated_bet_count)
  position_size = min(position_size, 0.02 × bankroll)  # hard cap

6.4 SIMULTANEOUS CORRELATED BETS
----------------------------------
RESEARCH: Wizard of Odds: "Props from the same game are correlated; if you bet
multiple props from the same game, you're taking on additional risk."

For PITCHER K PROPS specifically:
  - Same park, same umpire, same weather conditions: bets on pitchers in
    doubleheaders or twin bills are correlated (estimated ρ ≈ 0.15–0.25)
  - Different parks, different umpires: near-independent (ρ ≈ 0.05)

Stokastic/OddsShopper portfolio analysis: "When making 4 simultaneous Kelly-
sized bets, your bankroll experiences 2× the standard deviation of a single bet."

Markowitz portfolio theory applied to betting: diversifying across different
parks, weather conditions, and umpires reduces per-bet variance without reducing
expected return. Same-day diversification IS effective when bets are structurally
independent (different parks, different game conditions).

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION: 5-pitcher slate:
  - All in same city (doubleheader + evening game): treat as 2 independent units
  - Different parks in different cities: treat as 5 independent units
  - Apply √N portfolio adjustment to Kelly

    with hard 2% cap protects bankroll during high-variance periods.
  - SEVERITY: Medium

RISK: Market evolves and closes player prop inefficiency
  - Player prop markets are becoming more efficient as analytics firms and sharp
    syndicates focus on them. The current +0.54% CLV may compress.
  - MITIGATION: Track CLV monthly. If CLV falls below +0.2% over 200 bets,
    investigate whether market has adjusted. Feature improvement (umpire,
    framing) provides a new information edge that takes time for market to
    incorporate.
  - SEVERITY: Medium-Low (2–3 year horizon risk)

RISK: Weather API failure or umpire data gap
  - If weather data is unavailable day-of, model operates without weather features.
  - MITIGATION: Fallback to park historical climatology averages (May average
    temperature, etc.). Model was built without weather features and was valid.
  - SEVERITY: Low

8.3 RESEARCH RISKS
--------------------
RISK: NGBoost distributional calibration worse than current Poisson CDF
  - Distributional models can produce poorly-calibrated tails even when median is
    accurate. NB distribution may not be correct for all pitcher types.
  - MITIGATION: Test NB vs. Normal vs. Poisson distribution in NGBoost; use Brier
    score and CRPS as primary metrics; keep Poisson CDF as fallback ensemble
    component.
  - SEVERITY: Low-Medium

RISK: Umpire zone shrinkage (2026 structural change) makes 2023–2025 umpire
  data less predictive
  - 2026 shows 8% smaller strike zone vs. 2025. If umpire-individual rank order
    is stable but absolute rates shift, the features remain valid (relative
    comparison is what matters). If rank order changes (new umpires, different
    evaluation standards), historical umpire data is noisier.
  - MITIGATION: Use relative metrics (umpire_k_adj vs. league_avg) rather than
    absolute CSR. Re-calibrate umpire feature using 2025–2026 data only.
  - SEVERITY: Low-Medium
